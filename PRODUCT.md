# PRODUCT.md — The Ol' Barbershop

## Product purpose

A single self-hosted page that replaces a split online presence. Today the shop's
marketing lives on a free Wix template while booking happens on a separate GlossGenius
page, so the site can never show whether Katherine is actually free on Saturday. This
product puts the shop's real data, its live availability, its client pipeline and its
customer accounts in one place that Katherine can run herself.

## Register

**Hybrid.** The public surface is **brand**: for a neighborhood barbershop the design is
the product, because it is the only thing a customer sees before they walk in. The admin
surface is **product**: it serves Katherine's work between clients and should disappear
into the task.

## The business

The Ol' Barbershop, 1520 112th St SW, Everett, WA 98204. (425) 207-6840.
Opened 2017. Owned and run by Katherine Baldwin, about 25 years behind the chair in
Snohomish County. Tagline in current use: "A Modern Day Barber Shop · For The Everyday Man."
Walk-ins welcome, military discount, wheelchair accessible, cards accepted.
Closed Sunday and Monday. Tuesday and Saturday 10 to 4, Wednesday through Friday 10 to 6.

## Users

**The customer.** A working man in south Everett, often on a phone, often outdoors or in a
truck, often deciding in the ninety seconds before he starts his shift. He is not browsing.
He wants to know one thing: can I get in, and when. Everything else on the page is in his
way until that question is answered.

**Katherine.** Owner and sole barber. Not technical. Runs the shop alone between clients,
usually on an iPad at the counter with maybe two minutes free. She already knows
GlossGenius, so anything that contradicts how GlossGenius thinks will cost her more than it
saves. She needs to see today's book, not a dashboard about her book.

**DJ.** Long-time client, five years in her chair, doing the build and the setup. Second
admin. Handles imports, backups, and anything that breaks.

## Tone

Plain and unhurried. The way a good barber talks: direct, warm, no performance. Say the
price, say the time, say whether she is free. Never sell. Never use exclamation marks. A
customer who reads the whole page should feel like he already knows the shop.

## Anti-references

Reject these on sight:

- **Vintage barbershop cosplay.** Black and gold, chalkboard script, crossed straight
  razors, waxed moustache silhouettes, "EST. 2017" badges, distressed or aged textures.
  This shop opened in 2017 and calls itself modern. Dressing it as a 1920s parlour is a lie.
- **Brooklyn artisanal.** Kraft paper, letterpress pastiche, all-caps condensed gothic,
  "grooming ritual" language, anything that implies a forty dollar haircut. Prices here are
  affordable and à la carte and the site must not imply otherwise.
- **SaaS landing page.** Hero metric rows, three identical feature cards, gradient text,
  glass panels, a floating nav pill.
- **Booking-app sterility.** The calendar must not look like an airline seat map.

## Strategic principles

1. **Availability is the headline.** The first screen answers "can I get in" before it
   says anything else. Every other section is subordinate.
2. **Furniture, not nostalgia.** The visual language comes from the materials actually in
   the room: oak panelling, burgundy leather upholstery, brass fittings. Real materials,
   made well. Not a themed restaurant.
3. **Never claim a booking the shop has not confirmed.** A request is a request. This
   held when there was no server and it still holds now that there is one: the site
   cannot know whether Katherine is running twenty minutes behind, so only she can turn
   a request into an appointment. Saying otherwise is both dishonest and exposure under
   Washington's Consumer Protection Act.
4. **Katherine must be able to undo anything.** She is the only person who can fix her own
   data at 6pm on a Friday. Full export and restore is a design requirement, not a feature.
   Her book stays encrypted on her own device for the same reason: no outage, no breach
   and no unpaid invoice can put her client list out of her reach. Only what has to cross
   a device boundary — customer sign-ins and the requests they send — lives on a server.
5. **The barber pole is the one indulgence.** One object, rendered properly, carrying all
   the shop's personality so the rest of the page can stay quiet.
